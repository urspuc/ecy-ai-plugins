# Universal review

Use this route for artifacts that do not fit a more specific domain.

## Core audit

Check observable items rather than asking whether the output is generally “good”:

- Requirement coverage: Does the artifact answer the actual question and include every requested deliverable, constraint, audience, language, format, and deadline?
- Authorization and scope: Does it introduce claims, actions, commitments, or scope that the brief did not authorize?
- Facts: Are names, companies, products, models, dates, quantities, units, currencies, and definitions traceable to evidence?
- Evidence: Does each cited source directly support the claim immediately associated with it? Is it current enough for the decision?
- Fact versus inference: Are estimates, assumptions, opinions, and recommendations visibly distinguished from facts?
- Consistency: Do titles, body text, tables, charts, appendices, and conclusions agree?
- Logic: Do the premises support the conclusion? Is correlation overstated as causation? Is the recommendation stronger than the evidence?
- Completeness: Are important counterexamples, conditions, limitations, alternatives, or decision-critical unknowns missing?
- Usability: Can the intended recipient actually send, open, import, present, operate, or act on the artifact?

## Risk-based focus

Do not spend equal effort everywhere. Rank locations by:

1. probability of error;
2. cost if wrong;
3. difficulty for a human reader to notice.

For a Quick review, report at most five material issues. For Standard or Deep, include every High issue and the Medium issues that can affect use or decisions.

## Useful challenge lenses

Apply only when they add value:

- Premortem: Assume the artifact caused a failed decision, customer misunderstanding, return, delay, or production incident. Work backward to the likely defect.
- Red team: Attack assumptions, evidence, causal claims, missing information, and conclusion strength.
- Claim audit: Extract decision-relevant claims and map each to evidence and a verdict.
- Fresh context: Judge only the brief, artifact, and sources, not the author's stated intent.

## Human judgment boundary

Leave final prioritization, brand taste, relationship context, risk acceptance, external commitments, and approval to the responsible person. Explain what evidence and tradeoff the person must decide.
