# Communication, marketing and localization review

Choose the relevant section. Use the prior message, original-language content, brand guidance, and authorization boundaries as evidence when available.

## External email and business communication

First map every question, concern, fact, and requested action in the incoming message to the draft response. Check:

- whether each question is answered accurately and at the right level of certainty;
- names, companies, products, markets, dates, attachments, and prior-conversation context;
- price, quantity, MOQ, sample, lead time, shipping, discount, warranty, exclusivity, meeting, or resource commitments;
- whether “possible”, “can discuss”, or “pending confirmation” was upgraded to “we can provide”, “confirmed”, or “we will arrange”;
- whether internal views, sensitive information, or non-forwardable language was exposed;
- tone for the relationship stage and cultural context;
- unnecessary AI-style pleasantries, repetition, or over-explanation;
- whether the recipient knows the next step, owner, information required, and expected timing.

For every suspected commitment, classify it as authorized, unauthorized, or `UNABLE TO VERIFY`, and point to both the draft phrase and the supporting authorization when present.

## Marketing, copy and social

Do not give an overall score. Check:

- one identifiable audience and the situation or need being addressed;
- a visible value proposition linked to a real feature, evidence, or use case;
- specificity: who, what, where, why, and how rather than generic quality claims;
- claim support, comparisons, performance statements, guarantees, and superlatives;
- positioning and consistency with the supplied brand voice;
- platform, market, length, and format fit;
- a specific and appropriate call to action;
- repetition, empty professionalism, vague subjects, filler transitions, generic AI phrasing, and sentences that do not advance awareness, interest, trust, or action.

Flag “professional but information-free” sentences and name the missing ingredient: audience, situation, evidence, differentiator, or action.

## Translation and localization

Compare source and target line by line when both are available. Check:

- mistranslation, omission, unsupported addition, changed degree of certainty, and changed responsibility;
- approved glossary and consistency of product, feature, hardware, UI, and key names;
- numbers, units, currencies, dates, dimensions, model names, shortcuts, and symbols;
- register, relationship, formality, locale, and manual or UI suitability;
- target-language length, line break, button, label, table, and layout risk;
- cross-document consistency for recurring terms.

For uncertain terminology, provide:

| Source term | Current translation | Possible issue / alternative | Confidence | Context needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Do not resolve a domain term by confidence alone. State whether confirmation requires product behavior, UI location, target market, audience, glossary, or subject-matter-owner input.

## Human judgment boundary

People retain authority over commitments, negotiation strategy, relationship temperature, final brand voice, cultural naturalness, and whether a message should be sent.
